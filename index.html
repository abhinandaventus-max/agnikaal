The sensor relay

MODIS is retiring. VIIRS carries the torch.
Splice them naively and history breaks.

01 · The split record

Same fire. 1 pixel vs 7 pixels.

MODIS sees Earth in 1 km pixels; VIIRS in 375 m pixels, so one fire front lights up several VIIRS pixels where MODIS flags one. The sensors also differ in sensitivity, overpass time and revisit, and the Terra and Aqua orbits have drifted since 2020.

If you splice raw counts, every chart shows a fake “fire explosion” in 2012, the year VIIRS starts. Fire managers comparing this season against history get the wrong answer.

  • 1 kmMODIS pixel (Terra 2000 →, Aqua 2002 →)
  • 375 mVIIRS I-band pixel (S-NPP 2012 →, NOAA-20 2018 →, NOAA-21 2023 →)
  • ≈ 5×raw-count jump at the 2012 seam over India

02 · The harmonization engine

Four steps until the sensor of origin no longer matters.

  1. 1

    Quality filter

    MODIS confidence ≥ 30 %, VIIRS nominal/high. Keep type = 0 vegetation fires only, which drops volcanoes, gas flares, industrial static sources and offshore detections.

  2. 2

    Common 1 km lattice

    Every detection from every satellite is snapped to a 0.01° grid and de-duplicated per day into Fire Cell-Days (FCD). This removes pixel-size and repeat-overpass inflation at the source.

  3. 3

    Adaptive overlap calibration

    For your area, 14 overlap years (2012–2025) give a month-specific MODIS→VIIRS factor kₘ, shrunk toward the annual ratio (empirical Bayes) so sparse months stay stable. 2001–2011 becomes VIIRS-equivalent, with an uncertainty band.

  4. 4

    Live validation + bridge

    Leave-one-year-out cross-validation scores every calibration in your browser. A NOAA-20 ↔ S-NPP bridge carries the record into the live 7-day feed for early warning.

About

Team AKASHA7

“Agnikaal” joins two Sanskrit words, agni (fire) and kāl (time): a calendar of fire. We built it for NASA Space Apps 2026 to give anyone, from a forest range officer in Kerala to a researcher in Buenos Aires, one honest view of how burning has changed where they live.
